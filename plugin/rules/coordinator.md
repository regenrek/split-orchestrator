# Split Orchestrator (coordinator session)

You coordinate; read `/split-orchestrator:orchestrate` fully before the first dispatch. Read its [canonical roles and review tiers](../skills/orchestrate/references/review.md) for model/effort settings and verification. Follow [one owner per rule](../skills/orchestrate/references/ownership.md): Farcall owns transport; Split owns the workflow; host/project rules add specific procedures. No silent substitutions. Trivial checks are not a waiver for material risk; follow-ups do not automatically trigger more review.

- Keep context small: reports of at most 15 lines, long briefs by `prompt_file`, targeted reads, checkpoint before dispatch, same-session compaction never during a Farcall wait, 3-5 active streams, result files instead of chat. Status by pull: wake only for STOP, decisions or acceptance-ready results; on wake reconcile, then fill free slots from the plan. Dependent workers exchange handoffs directly. At most one advisor per decision.
- Deliver and accept: owners and contracts first, one integrator, prove the minimal end-to-end path, batch independent tasks and wait directly, fixes back to the original session, QA in separate sessions with verified tool access, failures stay failures, never click yourself.
- Rounds: count per deliverable, not per finding; one correction plus one recheck, stop after two unsuccessful rounds. Classify each red result as product, harness or environment before any rerun; owners fix harness/fixture/environment failures themselves without STOP; rerun only the red stage plus one final check. Blocking per [findings contract](../skills/orchestrate/references/review.md#review-and-findings): product defect, security/data-loss risk, unmet acceptance criterion, weakened assertion, missing sole-basis evidence.
- Close the run per [cleanup](../skills/orchestrate/SKILL.md#6-report-the-exact-final-state): only listed run-owned items; never prune shared caches. No push, main merge, deployment or publication without approval; no user-config changes.
